1. Introduction

1.1 Project Overview

Banks and educational institutions alike rely heavily on objective decision-support systems to streamline complex assessments. In higher education, undergraduate computing students are faced with a fast-evolving industry offering multiple specialized career trajectories, including Software Engineering, Data Science / AI, Cybersecurity, Cloud / DevOps, and UI/UX Design.

Despite the availability of online information, students frequently receive generalized, periodic advising that fails to account for their verified coursework, competency gaps, or weekly time constraints. CareerSense AI is an explainable artificial intelligence decision-support platform designed for undergraduate computing students. The solution integrates three core AI methods:

  • Machine Learning Classification (K-NN & Decision Tree) to match student feature profiles to candidate career tracks.
  • Rule-Based Expert System to validate foundational module prerequisites and compute prioritized skill gaps with explainable reasoning.
  • A* Search Algorithm to construct an optimal, time-budgeted weekly upskilling roadmap.

1.2 Problem Statement

Undergraduate computing students lack an adaptive, personalized, and explainable advisory platform that correlates academic transcript results, technical skills, and weekly study budgets with industry career benchmarks. Existing manual counseling is episodic and subjective, leading students to discover critical skill gaps too late in their degree, resulting in inefficient learning and prerequisite violations.

2. Changes Made After Proposal

2.1 Changes to Project Scope

The core scope remains faithful to the approved Stage 1 proposal. However, during prototype development, several high-impact enhancements were introduced:

  • Dual-Engine ML Classification: Augmented K-NN with an interpretable Decision Tree reference model, calculating a blended alignment probability (70% K-NN + 30% Decision Tree).
  • Dynamic Skill Reassessment: Completed roadmap tasks now immediately update the student's skill record in SQLite and recalibrate the Career Readiness score in real time.
  • Automated Career Advisory Reports: Integrated a comprehensive report generator producing official, downloadable HTML/PDF advisory dossiers.
  • Primary Persona Specialization: Focused the benchmark evaluation scenario on the Demonstration Student Persona (Year 2 Data Science & Business Analytics undergraduate profile).

2.2 Finalization of AI Techniques

The three AI techniques have been finalized as follows:

  1. Machine Learning Classification: Predicts career track affinities across 5 classes with confidence scores.
  2. Rule-Based Expert System: Enforces academic prerequisites (e.g. Programming ≥ B-) and diagnoses prioritized skill gaps.
  3. A* Search Algorithm: Computes optimal learning paths over an activity DAG respecting weekly study budgets.

3.2 Workflow Explanation

The system workflow proceeds through the following sequential stages:

  • Step 1 – Authentication: User logs in; role-based access enforces student, advisor, or coordinator views.
  • Step 2 – Profile & Academics Entry: Student inputs degree, semester grades, and skill self-ratings.
  • Step 3 – Feature Normalization: Converts student records into a 36-dimensional standardized vector.
  • Step 4 – ML Prediction: K-NN and Decision Tree generate ranked career probabilities.
  • Step 5 – Prerequisite Verification: Rule engine validates foundational academic course thresholds.
  • Step 6 – Skill Gap Diagnosis: Rule engine compares student vs benchmark levels to identify High/Medium gaps.
  • Step 7 – Career Readiness Scoring: Composite score computed from Competency (50%), Academics (25%), Projects (15%), and Documents (10%).
  • Step 8 – A* Roadmap Optimization: Generates optimal activity sequence budgeted to student's weekly study hours.
  • Step 9 – Progress Logging: Student marks milestones as Completed, triggering live profile updates.
  • Step 10 – Decision Support & Reporting: Advisor inspects diagnostics and student exports formatted reports.

5.2 Rule-Based Expert System

The rule engine evaluates academic prerequisites and competency gaps deterministically. Predefined rules include:

  • RULE_ACAD_DS_MATH: IF Target = 'Data Science / AI' AND Math Grade < B- THEN Flag Prerequisite Violation.
  • DEP_ML_PYTHON: IF Skill('Machine Learning') > None AND Skill('Python Data Stack') < Intermediate THEN Flag Prerequisite Dependency Gap.

5.3 A* Search Algorithm for Roadmap Optimization

Formulates learning path generation as graph search over 21 curated activities:

  • Cost $g(n)$: Accumulated learning effort in hours.
  • Heuristic $h(n)$: Sum of minimum hours required to bridge all unsatisfied skill gaps (strictly admissible, $h(n) \le h^*(n)$).
  • Scheduling: Maps activities into week-by-week milestones tailored to the student's study capacity (e.g. 8 hrs/week).

7. Problems Faced During Development

  1. Multi-Dimensional Feature Representation: Standardizing differing academic grades, interest scales (1–5), and skill levels (0–3) resolved via StandardScaler pipeline.
  2. Heuristic Admissibility in A* Search: Resolved by computing $h(n)$ as the strict minimum duration across uncompleted candidate activities.
  3. Transparent Prerequisite Rule Chaining: Forward-chaining evaluation maps subject areas dynamically to historical grades to produce actionable remediation advice.
  4. Dynamic State Management in Streamlit: Decoupled business logic into SQLite to preserve roadmap milestone updates across page re-runs.
  5. Multi-Class Evaluation Balance: Evaluated models using weighted precision, recall, and confusion matrices rather than simple accuracy.
